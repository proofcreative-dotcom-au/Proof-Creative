Insights / 05 – AI-TO-REALITY

How to tell whether an AI packaging concept can actually be made

A generated concept can be genuinely good and still be unmanufacturable. These are the six tests worth running before anyone commits budget, tooling or a launch date to it.

CARL CURCURUTO · PROOF CREATIVE

Written by Carl Curcuruto · Founder, Proof Creative · 14 years at Stranger & StrangerEditorial insight · Proof Creative

01

The structure test

Does the pack exist as an object?
THE TELL

Generated packs routinely show geometry no mould makes: a shoulder that could not be blown, a neck finish matching no standard closure, a label wrapping a compound curve with no distortion at all.

THE TEST

Name the container. Is it stock or custom? If it is stock, find the actual spec sheet and compare. If it is custom, establish who tools it, at what volume, and what that costs before anyone falls in love with it.

02

The flat test

Can it be unwrapped?
THE TELL

AI generates in perspective, which means anything hidden around the curve has never been designed. The back of the pack usually does not exist.

THE TEST

Try to reconstruct the flat: the dieline, the label shape, the repeat. If you cannot lay it flat without inventing a third of it, you are holding a picture of a pack rather than a design for one.

03

The reproduction test

Can each element be rebuilt and reused?
THE TELL

Logos are the tell. Generated marks are subtly inconsistent: asymmetric where they should be symmetric, stroke weight drifting across letterforms, a serif on one character and not the next. Generated lettering is not a typeface, so it cannot set a word it was not born with.

THE TEST

Set the product name in that look. Then set a second SKU name in it. If the second name cannot be made to match, the concept has no identity yet, only one image of one word.

04

The colour test

Is that colour available on that material?
THE TELL

Renders show colours that only exist in light: fluorescing brights, backlit glows, metallic sheens that are a lighting effect rather than an ink or a foil.

THE TEST

Name a Pantone against the actual substrate, not against the screen. If nothing matches, the concept depends on an effect, and you now have a decision to make about which process delivers it and what it adds to the unit cost.

05

The embellishment test

Can these finishes coexist?
THE TELL

Generated concepts stack foil, emboss, soft-touch and spot gloss on the same area without cost, because in a render none of them have tolerances.

THE TEST

Count the processes. Ask the printer what they can hold in register on this substrate, at this size, at this run length. Expect to lose one or two, and decide which ones carry the idea before the printer decides for you.

06

The compliance test

Is there room for the real information?
THE TELL

AI packs almost never carry the legal furniture: volume, ABV, allergens, barcode, country of origin, recycling marks, importer details, and the same again in a second language for export.

THE TEST

Place all of it, at real size, in the real positions. A surprising number of concepts do not survive this, and it is far better learned now than after tooling.

THE THREE OUTCOMES
01
Buildable as it stands
Rare, and usually only when the concept is simple: one colour, stock container, no embellishment stack. Worth checking rather than assuming.
02
Buildable, with rebuilding
The common outcome. The idea holds, but the assets do not exist yet. Identity, type, colour and structure all need to be genuinely built rather than traced.
03
Not buildable as shown
The concept depends on the render. Something central to its appeal, a material, a finish, a light, cannot be manufactured. This is not a dead end, but it does need an honest conversation before money is committed.

A failed test is not a wasted concept

A concept that fails several of these is still useful. It has told you what the brand wants to feel like, which is the hardest thing to get out of a written brief. What it has not done is any of the deciding. Every judgement that makes a pack manufacturable, which colour is a special, where the varnish holds back, what the minimum line weight can be, how the design adapts to the fourth SKU, is still waiting to be made by somebody.

The mistake is treating a generated image as a finished design and moving straight to artwork. The tolerances that govern foil, emboss and fine linework are negotiable at design stage and fixed by the time artwork starts, so the assessment has to happen first, while the concept can still change.

AI is very good at showing you what a pack could feel like. It has no idea what a pack has to survive.
