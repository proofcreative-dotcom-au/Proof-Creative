How premium spirits packaging moves from concept to shelf

Eight stages, in the order they have to happen. Almost every expensive failure on a premium pack is a stage done out of sequence.

CARL CURCURUTO · PROOF CREATIVE
01

Positioning and brief

What the liquid is, who it is for, what it costs, which markets it enters. These decisions set the ceiling on everything downstream, because a pack cannot rescue an unclear proposition.

WHERE IT SLIPS

Volumes and budget are left vague, so the design gets drawn without anyone knowing what the run can actually afford.

02

Structure and container

Stock bottle or custom glass. Closure, capsule and the label real estate all follow from this one choice, and custom glass is almost always the longest lead item on the project.

WHERE IT SLIPS

The container gets chosen after the label is designed, which inverts the dependency and forces the design to be redrawn.

03

Design

The creative work: hierarchy, typography, illustration, colour, and the material and finish intent that goes with it.

WHERE IT SLIPS

Everything is resolved on one flat front-label visual, with the back label, neck label and export variants deferred to somebody else later.

04

Approval

The handover point, and the most important line in the whole sequence. Before it, the question is what the pack should be. After it, the question is how it holds up.

WHERE IT SLIPS

Approval is given on a render nobody has interrogated for manufacturability, so the constraints arrive after the decision instead of before it.

05

Realisation

Dielines at 1:1, adaptation across every SKU and format, separations, embellishment layers built to real tolerances, and all the compliance furniture placed at real size.

WHERE IT SLIPS

It gets treated as file preparation rather than decision-making, and handed to whoever is cheapest and least senior.

06

Specification and suppliers

Substrate, processes, finishes and tolerances specified, then matched to a printer and converter who can actually hold them.

WHERE IT SLIPS

The job goes to the lowest quote without anyone matching what the pack demands to what that press can do.

07

Proofing

Wet proofs on the real stock, finished samples with the actual laminate and varnish, checked under the light the pack will live in.

WHERE IT SLIPS

The schedule was built without proofing time, so proofing becomes a formality rather than a decision point.

08

Press and shelf

First-off approval, attendance where the job warrants it, then filling, labelling, distribution, and the only test that counts.

WHERE IT SLIPS

Nobody senior is in the room at the moment the decision that matters actually gets made.

The sequence is the discipline

None of these stages are optional, and none of them can be swapped around without cost. Choosing glass after the label, approving before assessing, specifying after quoting, proofing after the schedule has been eaten: each one is survivable on its own, and expensive in combination.

A premium pack is not made premium by the finishes on it. It is made premium by the number of decisions that were taken deliberately, in the right order, by somebody who knew what came next.
