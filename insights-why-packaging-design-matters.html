Insights / 01 – BRAND & PACKAGING

Why Does Packaging Design Matter for My Product?

Good packaging does more than look the part. It communicates value, influences buying decisions and helps turn a good product into a commercially successful one.

CARL CURCURUTO · FOUNDER, PROOF CREATIVE

Written by Carl Curcuruto · Founder, Proof Creative · 14 years at Stranger & StrangerEditorial insight · Proof Creative

By Carl Curcuruto
Proof Creative

When you're developing a new product or growing an existing brand, packaging can feel like another expense on an already long list.

But packaging is often the first physical interaction someone has with your brand. Before they taste your spirit, experience your fragrance or try your skincare product, they're forming an impression based on what they see.

The question isn't simply whether your packaging looks good.

It's whether your packaging is working hard enough for your business.

01

First impressions have commercial value

Your packaging needs to communicate what your product is, who it's for and why it's worth choosing.

For premium products, this is particularly important. Customers are often being asked to pay more, and the packaging needs to help justify that positioning.

Consider two products sitting alongside each other. Both might be exceptional, but one communicates quality, character and confidence more effectively.

That difference can influence which product gets noticed, picked up and ultimately purchased.

Good packaging design brings together brand identity, typography, materials, structure and finishing to create a coherent impression.

Premium packaging isn't necessarily about spending more. It's about making every detail count.

02

Design for the customer, not just the presentation

A beautiful packaging concept is a starting point, not the finished solution.

It needs to work in the real world.

Can customers quickly identify the product and understand its benefits? Does it stand out against competitors? Is the information clear and easy to navigate?

And importantly, can the design be manufactured consistently at the right cost?

A metallic finish might look impressive on screen but add unnecessary expense. A beautifully detailed illustration might lose its impact when printed at actual size. A custom structure might look incredible but create complications during assembly or distribution.

These considerations shouldn't compromise creativity. They should inform it.

At Proof Creative, we connect packaging design with design realisation, considering materials, print processes and production requirements from the outset.

The best packaging is where creative ambition and commercial reality work together.

03

The true cost of packaging isn't just the design fee

One of the most overlooked aspects of packaging development is the relationship between upfront design investment and ongoing production costs.

A design fee is generally a one-off investment. Packaging materials, finishes and manufacturing costs repeat with every unit produced.

For example, adding a finish that costs an extra $0.35 per unit creates an additional $7,000 in production costs across 20,000 units.

That finish might be entirely justified if it strengthens the product's positioning and supports a better commercial outcome.

But it should be a considered decision.

Experienced packaging designers understand where investment creates value and where unnecessary complexity can be removed without compromising the result.

This is particularly important for growing brands, where relatively small decisions can have significant implications as production volumes increase.

04

Good packaging should grow with your brand

A successful product rarely stays as a single SKU forever.

New flavours, expressions, sizes, seasonal editions and gift packs all create additional packaging requirements.

Without a considered design system, each new product can become another standalone design exercise, creating inconsistencies and unnecessary costs.

A well-developed packaging system provides the flexibility to grow while maintaining a recognisable brand identity.

It establishes how typography, colour, product hierarchy and brand assets work together across different formats.

For established brands, this doesn't necessarily mean starting again.

Sometimes the greatest value comes from refining what already exists, improving the packaging architecture or extending a successful design across new products.

Good packaging design should support where your business is going, not just where it is today.

05

How do you measure the return on packaging design?

Packaging design can contribute to commercial performance in several ways:

  • Helping customers recognise and understand your product.
  • Supporting premium positioning and perceived value.
  • Improving consistency across products and retail environments.
  • Reducing avoidable artwork, printing and production errors.
  • Making future product launches and packaging rollouts more efficient.

But a better-looking pack doesn't automatically guarantee increased sales.

To assess the return, consider the full investment, including design, artwork, sampling, tooling and changeover costs, alongside any changes to ongoing unit costs.

Then measure the outcomes that matter: sales performance, contribution margin, production efficiency and the cost of developing future products.

For example, if a packaging project costs $12,000 and generates an additional $12 in contribution per unit sold, it would need 1,000 genuinely additional sales to recover that investment, assuming no other changes in costs or contribution.

That is a simplified illustration, not a forecast. The real calculation should also account for changes in the cost of existing sales, pricing, distribution and other commercial factors.

The important point is that packaging design should be treated as a business investment with clear objectives, rather than judged on appearance alone.

06

When should you invest in better packaging?

You don't necessarily need a complete rebrand.

It may be time to review your packaging if:

  • Your product quality has improved, but its presentation hasn't kept pace.
  • You're moving into premium retail or entering new markets.
  • Your range has grown without a consistent packaging system.
  • Your existing artwork creates recurring production challenges.
  • You're preparing to launch a new product, format or limited edition.

The right solution might be a targeted packaging refresh, a new design direction or specialist support to bring an existing concept into production.

Understanding the problem before deciding on the scope is an important part of protecting your investment.

07

From creative thinking to commercial reality

The most successful packaging isn't simply the most elaborate or expensive.

It's packaging that understands the product, communicates its value and can be delivered consistently in the real world.

Whether you're launching something new, refreshing an existing range or need experienced support to take an approved concept into production, the objective is the same.

Create packaging that looks right, works properly and makes commercial sense.
