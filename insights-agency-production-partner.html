When a design agency needs a white-label packaging production partner

Five signals that a packaging project has moved beyond what the studio can carry, and what to look for in the person you bring in.

THE SIGNALS
You have won the work and the production capability is one freelancer deep
The creative is strong, the client is committed, and the person who can actually take it to press is not on the payroll.
The range has outgrown the team
One SKU was manageable. Eight, across three formats and two markets, is a system, and systems need somebody whose job is consistency.
The pack needs processes nobody in the studio has specified
Foil registration, ceramic decoration, metallised substrates and screen print onto glass all behave in ways you learn by doing, not by reading.
The client is asking for specification and supplier liaison
That is production management, not artwork, and quoting it as artwork is how agencies lose money on packaging.
Your senior designers are being eaten by amends
Every hour a senior designer spends on regulatory copy updates is an hour not spent on the work you are actually known for.

What the role actually covers

Realisation of the approved creative across every SKU and format, production artwork built to specification, print and finish specification, supplier liaison, proofing, and press attendance where the job warrants it. Delivered under your brand, to your timelines, as part of your team.

What it is not is a second creative opinion. The design is yours and it stays yours. The job is to get it through manufacture intact.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR
01
Production credentials, not design credentials
You are not buying creative. You are buying the ability to protect creative through manufacture.
02
Someone who talks in tolerances
If the conversation is all aesthetics and no numbers, they are a designer, not a realisation partner.
03
Willingness to say no
A partner who agrees to everything will let you walk into a reprint. You want the person who flags the foil-to-varnish butt join before it is approved.
04
Respect for the creative intent
The point of the role is to keep the idea intact, not to sand it down to whatever is easiest to print.
